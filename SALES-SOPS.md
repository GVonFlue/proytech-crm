Six pages. Each one covers a single thing you do. None of them is longer than a
page, on purpose — a process you have to search through is a process nobody
follows.

- **SOP-01** Running a calling block — how a call day actually goes
- **SOP-02** Logging and dispositions — what gets written down, and why it
  matters more than it looks
- **SOP-03** Booking and handoff — the ninety seconds after someone says yes
- **SOP-04** Follow-up, callbacks and the send-only pile
- **SOP-05** Escalation — what you hand to Logan instead of answering
- **SOP-06** How you get paid

## One thing to be clear about up front

You are an independent contractor, not an employee. Nothing in here sets your
hours, requires you to work particular days, or stops you from working with
other companies. Where a page says "recommended" or "what works," that is
exactly what it means — it is the method we have seen produce results, offered
to you because it will make you money faster. How you actually run your day is
yours to decide.

What we do hold you to is the outcome and the standard: qualified appointments
that show up, logged honestly, handed over cleanly, and nothing said on a call
that we would not stand behind.

# SOP-01 · Running a calling block

*How a productive call day goes, and what the numbers should look like while you
are learning.*

## The numbers, honestly

Logan books **one appointment per thirteen dials**. He has been doing this a
while and it is a genuinely good rate. Expect **one per twenty-five to thirty in
your first two weeks**, improving toward **one per fifteen to eighteen by week
four**.

Your first ten days will produce almost nothing. That is not a warning, it is
the normal shape of this. Nobody is drawing conclusions about you in week one.

## Before the block — fifteen minutes

- List is loaded and sorted before you dial the first number. Never start a
  block by building a list — it kills the hour.
- Script and the qualification questions in front of you. Not open in a tab. In
  front of you.
- Water, phone charged, door shut, notifications off.

## The block itself

- **Dial in sets of twenty with no stopping.** Momentum is most of this — the
  fourth call is always better than the first.
- **Log the disposition code the second you hang up.** Five seconds. The full
  note goes in between sets.
- Five minutes between sets. Stand up, walk somewhere, do not check your phone.
- Two sets is a block — roughly sixty to ninety minutes. Two blocks is a full
  calling day.
- **Never dial the same number twice inside one block.**

## When people actually pick up

- **Service businesses** — roofing, HVAC, auto, landscaping: 8 to 10am and 4 to
  6pm. Midday they are on jobs.
- **Agents and lenders**: 9 to 11am and 1 to 3pm. Avoid weekends — they are
  showing.

This is what has worked, not a schedule you are being handed. If you find better
hours, take them and tell Logan so we can use it.

## Ending the block

1. Every row logged and complete. No blanks, no "will fill in later."
2. Every booking texted to Logan — see SOP-03.
3. Anything marked **HV** or **SO** handed to Logan and Garrett the same day.
4. One line to Logan: dials, conversations, bookings. Takes ten seconds and it
   is how we spot a problem in week two instead of week five.

# SOP-02 · Logging and dispositions

*Every dial gets a row before the next dial. This is the part that looks like
admin and is actually the whole thing.*

## Why this one matters more than it looks

At day fourteen we sit down and decide whether this is working. If the log is
honest, we can tell whether the problem is the list, the opener, or the offer —
and fix the right one. If the log is thin or flattering, we cannot tell
anything, and the conversation becomes about you instead of about the process.
Log the bad calls exactly as carefully as the good ones.

## The eight codes

| Code | Means | What has to be true |
|---|---|---|
| **NA** | No answer | Rang out. No voicemail left. Attempt count goes up by one. |
| **VM** | Voicemail left | First attempt only. Never leave a second one. |
| **CB** | Callback | They gave you a specific day and time. Write the exact time, not "next week." |
| **NF** | Not a fit | Disqualified on the call. The reason goes in the row — no blank reasons. |
| **SO** | Send only | Wanted the link, would not book. Handed to Logan and Garrett the same day. |
| **BK** | Booked | Time agreed, contact details captured, Logan texted. |
| **HV** | High value | Bigger operation. Handed to Logan and Garrett, never worked yourself. |
| **DNC** | Do not call | They asked not to be contacted. Permanent. Tell Logan the same day. |

## What goes in the row

- Date · business name · contact name · phone · industry
- Disposition code
- **Their words** — what they said to the hook question, quoted, not summarised.
  "We just let it go to voicemail" is useful. "Not great at follow-up" is not.
- Any objection you hit, and what you said back — including the ones you
  handled. Especially those.
- Next action and the date it happens

## Three rules

1. **Write their words, not your read on them.** Your read changes; the quote
   does not.
2. **NF always carries a reason.** A disqualify with no reason is a lost lead
   dressed up as a decision.
3. **DNC is permanent and immediate.** No follow-up, no "one more try in a
   month," no exceptions. Tell Logan the same day.

If you catch yourself not wanting to log a call, that is the one worth logging.
Nobody has ever been in trouble here for a bad call. Being unable to explain a
bad week is a different problem.

# SOP-03 · Booking and handoff

*The ninety seconds after they say yes. Get this wrong and the appointment does
not happen.*

## On the call, in this order

1. **Lock the time.** "Thursday at ten, or Thursday at two?" Never offer an open
   calendar.
2. **The five things Logan needs to build it:** business name exactly as they
   want it written · their current website if they have one · the three things
   they want the phone ringing for · where they work, town or radius · where
   their photos live.
3. **Full name, spelled back to them. Email, read back. Mobile, read back.** All
   three, every time.
4. **Confirm the invite:** "You will get a calendar invite from Logan in the
   next few minutes. If it is not there in ten, check spam and text me."
5. **Set the frame:** "He will have yours up on the screen when you get on. Ten
   minutes, and there is nothing on the other side of it."

## Within ten minutes of hanging up

Text Logan, in one message: business name · contact name · phone · email · the
time booked · industry · the five things. Then log it **BK**.

Ten minutes is not a formality. Logan builds their site in the half hour before
the appointment — he cannot start until your text lands, and an appointment he
finds out about an hour late is an appointment he walks into cold.

## No-shows

- Send one text within fifteen minutes: "Hey [Name] — Logan is on and ready when
  you are, everything alright?"
- If nothing, one attempt to rebook later that day or the next.
- If they no-show twice, log **NF** and move on. Do not chase a second no-show —
  it is the clearest answer you will get.

## Reschedules and cancellations

- A reschedule is yours to fix. Same-day text, new time, re-confirm, re-text
  Logan.
- A cancellation gets one question: "Just timing, or is it not for you?" Timing
  goes to **CB** with a date. Anything else goes to **NF** with the reason.

# SOP-04 · Follow-up, callbacks and the send-only pile

*Most reps lose their week here — chasing people who already answered them.*

## Send only (SO)

They wanted the link and would not book. Take one swing at booking them the same
day using the discovery line in the script. If they still will not, hand it over
and stop.

- Text Logan and Garrett the same day: name, business, number, email.
- Log it **SO**. It leaves your list at that point.

This is not you giving up. It is a lead that needs a different conversation than
the one you are equipped to have, and it is worth more to us in Logan and
Garrett's hands than sitting in your follow-up pile for three weeks.

## Callbacks (CB)

- Call at the **exact time** they gave you. Not the same day, the time.
- Open differently: "Hey [Name] — you asked me to try you back this afternoon."
  That is a warm call now. Do not re-run the cold opener.
- If they blow off two agreed callback times, it is an **NF**. They have
  answered you.

## Not a fit (NF)

- Dead, unless the reason was timing. If it was timing, set a real date and let
  it go until then.
- Never re-work an NF because you had a slow week. That is how a list gets
  burned.

## The attempt cap

- **Three attempts on a cold number across two weeks, then it dies.** Voicemail
  on the first attempt only.
- Four or more calls to someone who has never picked up is not persistence, it
  is how we end up on a DNC list.

## Do not call

Immediate, permanent, no exceptions, and never a "well, they were just having a
bad day." Log **DNC**, tell Logan the same day, and it never gets dialled again.
This one is not a judgement call and there is no version of it where pushing
helps us.

# SOP-05 · Escalation

*What you hand to Logan instead of answering. Handing something over is never
the wrong call.*

## Escalate the same day, by text

- **A high-value prospect** — team lead, 25+ deals, multiple crews or trucks,
  more than one location, already running ads. Never work these yourself. Logan
  and Garrett take them.
- Any question about **contracts, refunds, guarantees, cancellation, or anything
  legal**.
- Anyone asking about **the CRM, the automation packages, or anything priced per
  person**. You never put a number on those.
- **Any request for a discount**, however small, however close they sound to
  saying yes.
- Someone who says they are **already a client**, or who has a complaint about
  us.
- **An angry call**, or anyone who asks not to be contacted again.
- Anything you would otherwise have to guess at.

## How to escalate without losing the call

> "That is a fair question, and I would rather get you the right answer than
> guess at it. Let me put Logan on it — can I grab you ten minutes with him?"

This makes you look better, not worse. A rep who says "I do not know, let me get
you someone who does" reads as honest. A rep who guesses reads as a salesman,
and they hear it instantly.

## What you do not escalate

Normal objections. "What is it cost," "I already have a website," "just send me
the link," "not interested" — those are yours, they are in the script, and
handling them is the job. Escalation is for things outside your authority, not
things that are hard.

## What to expect back

Logan answers within the business day. Do not hold a live call waiting on him —
book the appointment, escalate after, and let him sort it before the call
happens.

# SOP-06 · How you get paid

*One way you earn today, and what is likely coming. No invoices to submit, no
forms to chase.*

## Before your first payment

- Signed representative agreement
- Completed W-9
- Payment method on file

You are an independent contractor. Nothing is withheld from what you are paid,
and you will get a 1099-NEC after year end if you earn more than the IRS
threshold. What you owe in tax on it is yours to handle — worth setting some
aside from the first payment rather than the last.

## Commission — 25% of the build

- **25% of the collected build fee** on every deal you sourced. Nothing is taken
  off the top first — it is 25% of what the client actually paid for the build.
- The build runs $749 to $1,549 depending on size and how much automation goes
  behind it, so that is roughly **$187 to $387 a deal**.
- The monthly is **not** commissionable, and neither are the CRM or automation
  packages — those are priced per person and quoted by Logan.
- Paid on the **5th of the month** after the client's money clears.

## The three states

- **Pending** — the deal closed. Your commission is counted and visible, but it
  is not money yet.
- **Earned** — the client actually paid. This is the real state and it is what
  gets paid out.
- **Voided** — the client cancelled or never paid. It leaves your totals.

Commission follows collected money, not signed paper. A client who signs and
never pays is not a deal, and we are not going to pretend otherwise in either
direction — the day they pay, it is yours.

## What this does not include yet

Right now you are paid on closed deals only. There is no payment for booking an
appointment that does not convert. We expect to add something on held
appointments once there is enough volume to price it properly — when that
happens you will get it in writing before it starts, and it will be in addition
to the 25%, not instead of it. Until you see that in writing, assume it does not
exist.

## What you submit

Nothing. Logan reconciles commission against what actually landed in the bank,
and you will get a short statement with each payment showing which deals it
covers.

## If something looks wrong

Raise it within thirty days of the payment and we will go through it together
against the log. That is why SOP-02 exists — an honest log is the thing that
protects you here, not us.

## Your costs

You cover your own phone, internet and wherever you work from. You set your own
hours, you use your own equipment, and you are free to work with other companies
while you work with us.
